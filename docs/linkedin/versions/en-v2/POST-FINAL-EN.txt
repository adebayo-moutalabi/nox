Your ADHD brain isn't lazy.
It just needs a starter.

People tell you "just get organized." So you tried to-do lists, planners, apps. They all wait for you to take the first step. And that first step is exactly where you get stuck.

So I built Nox.

Nox is a little companion that lives on your desktop. It doesn't hand you yet another to-do list. It does what a friend sitting next to you would do:

→ It talks to you first. You tell it what you need to do, out loud.
→ It breaks everything into tiny steps of 2 to 10 minutes. You only see the next one.
→ It starts a visible timer, so time feels real again.
→ You drift off? It gently brings you back. No judgment.
→ Interrupted? When you come back: "You were at…". Back on track in 5 seconds.
→ Forgetting breaks? It suggests one, and saves your spot.
→ It remembers you: what blocks you, when you have energy, what helps. Everything stays on your PC.

Nothing here is random: the onboarding (5 questions, under a minute) and the 7 built-in methods (Pomodoro, body doubling, the 5-minute rule, "if… then…" plans) are grounded in ADHD and executive-function research.

I designed it for ADHD first. But if a notification, a coworker or a noise is enough to make you lose the thread, it's for you too.

Built for the OpenAI DevDay Community Hackathon in Paris, with the OpenAI Realtime API for smooth, natural voice conversations.

Nox is free, runs on Windows in English or French, and is ready in one minute.
